§7 · Diagnosis

It's a one-product problem, and the first session decides

63% of visitors view exactly one product, and they add to cart only 10.9% of the time. Browsing depth is the strongest correlate of activation in the data.

§7.1 · Recommendations

Three moves, judged on purchases

Play 1 · product pages

Turn one-product visits into multi-product visits

Add "pairs well with" and "complete the routine" modules plus quick-add buttons on product pages and listings. A/B test on landing product pages, since depth may be a symptom of intent rather than its cause.

Target: 1-product band 10.9% → 2-product band 18.3%
Play 2 · lifecycle

Re-engage the 126K high-intent browsers

Within 24 hours of a second session with 3+ product views and no cart, send an email or push featuring what they looked at. Keep a holdout group to measure true incrementality.

Pool: 126,129 known users, 10.2% of non-carters
Play 3 · measurement

Score every test on visit→purchase

Pre-register visit→purchase as the primary metric and treat cart rate as a diagnostic. An activation win only beats an equal checkout win if new carters buy at 25.5% or better. October's carters bought at 19.0%.

Baseline: 6.4–8.4% visit→purchase by month

Method

  • 1,109,098 exact duplicate rows removed, leaving 19,583,742 events.
  • No order ID exists, so an order is all purchase rows in one session. 155,617 orders, $6.35M revenue.
  • 911 purchasers with no cart event are excluded from Conversion by the subset rule.
  • Five months of data: the October cohort is visible to M4, February only to M0. Hatched cells can't be observed.
  • Opportunity counts one order per new buyer, a deliberately conservative choice.
